# Greek Opening Monologue — 60 seconds
### W2-T5 · feeds the demo at T+00:00

The pitch opens in Greek for the first 60 seconds. Reasons:

1. EYP officers are the audience. Speaking their language for the
   opening signals *"we built this for you, not for a generic
   intelligence market."*
2. The platform's brand-defining feature — Greek-native AoI naming
   ("Λεκάνη Λήμνου", "Βόρεια Ζώνη Ηρακλείου") — only lands if the
   judges hear Greek toponymy from the speaker's mouth before they
   read it on the screen.
3. It earns the speaker the right to switch to English for the rest of
   the demo without seeming like they're avoiding Greek.

---

## The text (memorise word-for-word, no notes on stage)

> *"Καλημέρα. Είναι Τρίτη πρωί. Σε ένα γραφείο της EYP, ένας αναλυτής μπαίνει
> στις οκτώ. Ανοίγει τον υπολογιστή του. Δεν πληκτρολογεί ερώτηση. Δεν
> γράφει ορισμούς. Κοιτάει τι έγινε ενώ κοιμόταν.*
>
> *Πριν από έξι ώρες, στις τρεις τη νύχτα, το Damocles ολοκλήρωσε την
> νυχτερινή σάρωση. Έξι Περιοχές Ενδιαφέροντος. Έξι κόκκινες. Σε μία από
> αυτές — τη Βόρεια Ζώνη Ηρακλείου — έχει ήδη γράψει την αναφορά: 'υψηλός
> κίνδυνος αναφοράς από μία μόνο πηγή, πιθανή λάθος γεωκωδικοποίηση,
> ζητήστε αμέσως αρχείο ραντάρ από την Ακτοφυλακή.'*
>
> *Η EYP δεν περίμενε ερώτηση. Δεν περίμενε να ξυπνήσει κάποιος. Και κάθε
> πρόταση αυτής της αναφοράς οδηγεί κρυπτογραφικά πίσω στην ίδια την πηγή
> της. Δεν είναι summary. Είναι chain.*
>
> *Στα επόμενα τέσσερα λεπτά θα σας δείξω τρία πράγματα. Πρώτο: πώς η
> πλατφόρμα μιλάει στον αναλυτή προτού της μιλήσει. Δεύτερο: πώς αμφισβητεί
> τον εαυτό της μέσα στην ίδια αναφορά. Τρίτο: πώς αποδεικνύει με
> κρυπτογραφία ότι δεν έχει αλλοιωθεί. Ξεκινάμε."*

## English back-translation (for the pitch deck speaker-notes)

> *"Good morning. It's Tuesday morning. In an EYP office, an analyst
> walks in at eight. He opens his computer. He doesn't type a question.
> He doesn't write definitions. He looks at what happened while he was
> asleep.*
>
> *Six hours ago, at three in the morning, Damocles finished the
> overnight scan. Six Areas of Interest. Six red. For one of them — the
> North Heraklion Zone — it has already written the brief: 'high risk
> of being a single-source report, possible geocoding error, request
> Coast Guard radar logs immediately.'*
>
> *EYP didn't wait for a question. It didn't wait for anyone to wake up.
> And every sentence of that brief traces cryptographically back to its
> source. It's not a summary. It's a chain.*
>
> *In the next four minutes I'll show you three things. First: how the
> platform speaks to the analyst before she speaks to it. Second: how it
> argues against itself inside the same brief. Third: how it proves with
> cryptography that nothing has been altered. Let's begin."*

## Coaching notes

**Cadence.** This is ~165 Greek words. At a deliberate intelligence-briefing
pace (~3 words/sec on a screen-supported pitch) that's 55 seconds. Aim for
**60 seconds end-to-end with two breath pauses** — one after "*ενώ
κοιμόταν*", one before "*Στα επόμενα τέσσερα λεπτά*".

**Pronunciation traps.**

- "κρυπτογραφικά" — say this with confidence. It's a long word and
  judges will be listening for the speaker's ease with technical Greek.
- "Damocles" — say it in Greek phonetics: **Δα-μο-κλής** is wrong (that's
  the historical figure). Use the brand pronunciation **Ντα-μό-κλες**
  (with stress on the second syllable, like the English "DAMM-uh-kleez").
- "Heraklion" — **Η-ρά-κλει-ο**, stress on the second syllable.

**Eye contact.** The opening sentence ("Καλημέρα.") goes to the panel chair.
The "Έξι Περιοχές Ενδιαφέροντος. Έξι κόκκινες." line goes to the technical
panellist. The closing line ("Ξεκινάμε.") goes to all of them, then to the
screen.

**Forbidden words.** Do not say *"AI"*. Do not say *"machine learning"*. Do
not say *"automation"*. These flatten the platform to a category EYP
already knows it doesn't want to buy from. The platform is called by
name: **Damocles**.

**On a stumble.** If you lose the thread, drop directly to: *"Σε ένα
γραφείο της EYP, ένας αναλυτής κοιτάει τι έγινε ενώ κοιμόταν."* The rest
flows from there.

## Rehearsal protocol (W4-T2)

Three full deliveries per day, three days minimum:

1. Day 1 — record audio, play back, hear what doesn't land.
2. Day 2 — record again with the screen running behind you. Watch
   where your eye drifts during the slow lines.
3. Day 3 — deliver to a Greek-native reader who is NOT a colleague.
   Ask them: "what's the platform's name and what does it do?" After
   60 seconds they should be able to answer both.

If at the end of day 3 they can't, the monologue gets re-written before
W4 closes. We do NOT step into the W4 dry-runs with this still wobbling.

---

## Why these three claims, in this order

The monologue makes three promises that the demo then keeps:

1. **"speaks before being spoken to"** → kept by the cold-open triage list.
2. **"argues against itself"** → kept by the Devil's Advocate moment at click 6.
3. **"proves with cryptography"** → kept by the tamper demo (W3-T1).

That's the three-promise contract. The Q&A panel can interrupt at any
point and the speaker can show, not tell. *"You asked about hallucination?
Watch the Devil's voice on this brief, written by the same system."*
